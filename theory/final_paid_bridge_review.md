At the same candidate/reference origin and prefix ledger, require common reduced-measurable readiness and feasibility `G`. Let `m_H=E[D|H]`, estimated paid mean `F_H−5`, and nonnegative issued penalty `q_H S_H`. Exact gate regret is the expectation of `G |m_H|` on states where its sign decision differs from the oracle. It is bounded by paid-mean estimation error plus the expected penalty. Consequently, the fitted retained gate beats every feasible reduced policy in expectation whenever its oracle information gap exceeds those two quantities.

The error term uses the true physical law. A numerical distance between fitted controls does not supply it. For the implemented target mean, the nonnegative service slack `δ=2−ell` must also enter paid-mean error. No independent-error assumption or calibrated-confidence premise is needed for the decision-theoretic bound; neither premise is established for the fitted working posterior.

The result is restricted to the same decision and ledger. Earlier admissions can change settled loss, unresolved reserve, future feasibility and delayed calibration state. Summing single-origin oracle gaps across those separate histories would be invalid; a sequential result requires continuation values or a coupling that keeps execution state common. The shared execution-loss bound remains a separate benefit available to all controllers.
