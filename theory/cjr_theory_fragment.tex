\paragraph{Effective-support normalization of mature paired blocks.}
Let $\mathcal E_k$ contain the eligible completely matured historical blocks
with at least one observed source coordinate.  Apply the existing absolute
relevance cutoff before forming $\mathcal E_k$.  For its positive whole-block
weights $w_j$, write
\begin{equation}
 S_k=\sum_{j\in\mathcal E_k}w_j,\qquad
 Q_k=\sum_{j\in\mathcal E_k}w_j^2,\qquad
 n_{\mathrm{eff},k}=S_k^2/Q_k,\qquad
 w'_j=(S_k/Q_k)w_j.
 \label{cjr_effective_support_normalization}
\end{equation}
CJ-R uses these same $w'_j$ in the complete-block predictive covariance,
masked correction precision and location, missing-coordinate completion and
paired mixture masses.  Every normalized information control makes the same
replacement.  Observed residuals, source masks and relative relevance are
retained; a zero-coordinate block contributes no effective error information.
An empty $\mathcal E_k$ retains the reference.

\begin{proposition}[Scale invariance at fixed eligible support]
\label{cjr_fixed_support_scale_invariance}
For $n=|\mathcal E_k|\geq1$ positive weights,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_jw'_j=n_{\mathrm{eff},k}\in[1,n],\qquad
 \frac{w'_j}{w'_\ell}=\frac{w_j}{w_\ell}.
 \label{cjr_normalized_mass_and_ratios}
\end{equation}
Multiplying every $w_j$ by the same $c>0$ leaves all $w'_j$ unchanged.
Hence the consistently rebuilt CJ-R law, target posterior and issued score
are invariant under this scaling when the eligible blocks, observed residuals,
masks, quality, current forecasts, configuration, calibration state and
execution ledger are fixed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The total mass is $S_k^2/Q_k$.  Positive cross-products give $S_k^2\geq Q_k$,
and Cauchy--Schwarz gives $S_k^2\leq nQ_k$.  The ratios cancel the common
multiplier.  Scaling maps $(S_k,Q_k)$ to $(cS_k,c^2Q_k)$, so each transformed
weight and every subsequent deterministic calculation remain unchanged.
\end{proof}
The invariance does not extend across the absolute eligibility cutoff.
For $p_j=w_j/S_k$, $n_{\mathrm{eff},k}=1/\sum_jp_j^2$ measures weight
concentration.  Independent homoscedastic scalar observations would give
weighted-mean variance $\sigma^2/n_{\mathrm{eff},k}$, whereas dependent blocks
give $p^{\mathsf T}\Gamma p$ for their covariance $\Gamma$.
Thus this mass is not a calibrated count of independent observations.
The prior-atom empirical share changes from $S_k/(a_0+S_k)$ to
$n_{\mathrm{eff},k}/(a_0+n_{\mathrm{eff},k})$.  This removes common kernel
attenuation but can amplify distant, weakly overlapping history; raw $S_k$,
$Q_k$, the multiplier and masks are retained in the audit.
Because the predictive covariance is also reestimated, no monotonic reduction
of target variance, or increase of admission score or paid return, follows.

\paragraph{A paid-action bridge for fitted retained information.}
Fix one candidate/reference lease and its complete integrable paid return $D$.
Let $\mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal I$ denote reduced and retained information,
both containing the current forecasts and an identical execution state.
Let $G\in\{0,1\}$ be their common $\mathcal R$-measurable feasibility indicator.
Write $M_{\mathcal H}=\mathbb E[D\mid\mathcal H]$ and
$V(\mathcal H)=\mathbb E[G(M_{\mathcal H})_+]$.
The oracle information increment is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=V(\mathcal I)-V(\mathcal R)
 =\mathbb E\!\left[G\min\!\left\{
 \mathbb E[(M_{\mathcal I})_+\mid\mathcal R],
 \mathbb E[(-M_{\mathcal I})_+\mid\mathcal R]\right\}\right]\geq0.
 \label{cjr_oracle_paid_information_gap}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Fitted paid-policy regret]
\label{cjr_fitted_paid_policy_regret}
For an $\mathcal H$-measurable estimate $\widehat M_{\mathcal H}$ and
penalty $r_{\mathcal H}\geq0$, issue
$A_{\mathcal H}=G\mathbf1\{\widehat M_{\mathcal H}-r_{\mathcal H}>0\}$.
Its regret satisfies
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Reg}_{\mathcal H}
 &:=V(\mathcal H)-\mathbb E[A_{\mathcal H}D]
 =\mathbb E[G|M_{\mathcal H}|\mathbf1\{
 \mathbf1\{\widehat M_{\mathcal H}-r_{\mathcal H}>0\}
 \ne\mathbf1\{M_{\mathcal H}>0\}\}],\nonumber\\
 \operatorname{Reg}_{\mathcal H}
 &\leq\mathbb E[G|\widehat M_{\mathcal H}-M_{\mathcal H}|]
       +\mathbb E[Gr_{\mathcal H}].
 \label{cjr_paid_regret_bound}
\end{align}
Against any feasible reduced-information policy $A_{\mathcal R}$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[(A_{\mathcal I}-A_{\mathcal R})D]
 \geq\Delta-\mathbb E[G|\widehat M_{\mathcal I}-M_{\mathcal I}|]
             -\mathbb E[Gr_{\mathcal I}].
 \label{cjr_sufficient_fitted_paid_gain}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition on $\mathcal H$.  The optimal action selects the positive $M$,
and subtracting the issued action gives the exact regret identity.
A harmful admission has $M\leq0<\widehat M-r$, so $|M|\leq|\widehat M-M|$.
A missed benefit has $M>0\geq\widehat M-r$, so
$|M|\leq|\widehat M-M|+r$.  Integrate and use
$\mathbb E[A_{\mathcal R}D]\leq V(\mathcal R)$.
\end{proof}
The CJ score uses $\widehat M=N\widehat\mu-5$ and
$r=qN\sqrt{\widehat v+\nu^2}$; the nonnegative service slack in
$D=NU-5+\delta$ must remain in paid-mean error or be counted as additional
conservatism.  The error term concerns the true physical conditional law,
not distance between two fitted controls.  Retained pairing can supply
$\Delta$, while estimation error and unnecessary penalties can consume it.
The result is a sufficient conditional statement, not a superiority or
accepted-action coverage guarantee.  Separate budget trajectories can have
different $G$ after different past actions; one cannot sum these fixed-state
gaps across them.  The common loss ledger supplies its separate pathwise bound.
