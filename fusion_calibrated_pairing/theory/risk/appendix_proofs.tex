\subsection{Immutable conditional score calibration: implementation and scope}
\label{app:conditional-immutable-calibration}
Let $\mathcal I_0$ be the completed score-fitting records from a
physical partition disjoint from state fitting, configuration selection
and test. Each physical origin enters once using a canonical delay seed.
Keep this complete library across participant/session restarts; its base
mass is $2/|\mathcal I_0|$ per record. Let $\mathcal I_j$ contain the
latest 48 online records whose complete feedback has matured by the
current issuance. For $K_b(z,z_i)=\exp\{-\|z-z_i\|^2/(2b^2)\}$,
the weights are
\begin{equation}
 w_{ji}=K_b(z_j,z_i)
 \begin{cases}
 2/|\mathcal I_0|,&i\in\mathcal I_0,\\
 0.97^{\operatorname{arrival\ age}(i)},&i\in\mathcal I_j.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:conditional-calibration-weights}
\end{equation}
Persistent fitting records are not assigned an artificial participant-local
age. The conditional empirical quantile is the first sorted residual
whose cumulative weight reaches $p\sum_iw_{ji}$, for
$b\in\{0.25,0.5,1\}$ and $p\in\{0.8,0.9,0.95\}$.
An empty archive gives correction zero and remains uncertified.
The effective weighted count is $(\sum_iw_{ji})^2/\sum_iw_{ji}^2$;
it measures weight concentration, not a number of independent subjects.

The live interface stores immutable $(S_j,N_j,z_j,k_j,m_j)$, checks
integer origin/maturity timestamps and complete-lease support, rejects
duplicate or premature callbacks, and reads $X_j$ only when $m_j$ has
arrived. Simultaneous callbacks are ordered by maturity and origin.
Out-of-order completed feedback is usable for future score calibration,
but a statistical admission-prefix report waits for the longest fully
completed origin prefix. The replay attaches offline coverage and return
fields only after causal scores and proposals are fixed. Whole, dedicated
selection sessions are evaluated in full; a within-session half split
requires an explicit, separately declared boundary. It cannot silently
replace the dedicated score-fitting partition.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional score validity under a valid error quantile]
\label{prop:conditional-error-quantile}
Let $\mathcal F_j$ contain the complete pre-admission information,
including the fitted law, history, calibration state and service reserve.
Suppose $S_j,N_j,q_j$ are $\mathcal F_j$ measurable, $N_j>0$,
$X_j\in[-N_j-5,N_j-5]$, and
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{(S_j-X_j)/N_j>q_j\mid\mathcal F_j\}\leq\tau.
 \label{eq:valid-conditional-residual-quantile}
\end{equation}
Then the clipped score in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-immutable-score}
has lower-target violation probability at most $\tau$ conditional on
$\mathcal F_j$. Any predictable readiness/reserve thinning preserves
that inequality for actually admitted leases. If $D_j\geq X_j$ and
$L_j>0$ is required for admission, harmful-return probability is also
at most $\tau$ on this same conditional event.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Before clipping, $X_j<S_j-N_jq_j$ is equivalent to
$(S_j-X_j)/N_j>q_j$. Clipping an overlarge score at the upper physical
support lowers it; clipping a score below the lower support cannot create
a violation because $X_j$ lies above that support. A predictable admission
event is measurable in $\mathcal F_j$, so multiplying the conditional
violation indicator by that event preserves the bound. Finally,
$D_j<0$ implies $X_j<0<L_j$, and hence a lower-target violation.
\end{proof}

This proposition is a sufficient condition. A locally weighted adaptive
quantile is not proved to satisfy it by its construction. In particular,
exchangeability of all-issued residuals alone does not give coverage after
selecting $L_j>0$, and a nominal $p=0.9$ is not empirical 90\% admission
coverage. Participant dependence, changing model versions and missing
feedback can change the relevant error law. The experiment therefore
reports actual admission coverage, exceedance, negative returns and the
shared budget separately, with no automatic future-risk certificate.

\paragraph{One finite-sample route to a stronger claim.}
A valid, stricter certificate requires additional sampling assumptions
and adequate support. Freeze the finite scoring family before the
calibration sample is observed, and predeclare $G$ strata. Suppose that
within each stratum the fixed-number score-fitting errors are IID from a
continuous CDF $F_g$, and the future error conditional on all additional
pre-admission inputs and past history has this same CDF. This last
sufficiency/stability condition is stronger than marginal exchangeability.
It must be justified by the intended environment; it is not asserted for
the participant-shift experiment. For $K$ candidate scorers, choose an
order $k$ in a stratum with $m$ observations such that
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)\geq k\}
 \leq\delta/(KG),\qquad q_g=e_{(k)}.
 \label{eq:conditional-tolerance-order}
\end{equation}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, every scorer/stratum then satisfies
$F_g(q_g)\geq p$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-error-quantile}
applies with $\tau=1-p$ after finite trial selection and a predictable
budget guard. To see this, $F_g(e_{(k)})<p$ implies at least $k$
calibration errors below the population $p$ quantile, whose count is
binomial. A union bound covers the declared family and strata.
The sample size is fixed before inspecting outcomes; the usual fixed-$n$
binomial calculation is not transferred to a budget-stopped, random
admission count. With zero calibration exceedances and $q_g$ equal to
the sample maximum, $p=0.9$, $\delta=0.05$ require at least 29 observations
for one scorer/stratum, and 50 for nine scorers/one stratum. More strata
require more independent evidence. The executed weighted, adaptive
procedure is not this IID tolerance certificate.

\paragraph{Anytime report for executed, dependent admissions.}
For a predeclared finite family of $K$ causal policies and a declared
bounded endpoint $Y_i\in[0,1]$, let
$r_i=\mathbb E[Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i^-]$, with the enlarged filtration
containing previous physically completed lease targets and current inputs.
For predeclared positive $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_J$, Ville's inequality
gives, simultaneously over methods and actual admission prefixes,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n r_i\leq
 \min\left\{1,\min_{j\leq J}
 \frac{\lambda_j\sum_{i=1}^nY_i+\log(KJ/\delta)}
 {(1-e^{-\lambda_j})n}\right\}
 \label{eq:conditional-score-admission-envelope}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta$. Its proof uses
$e^{-\lambda y}\leq1-(1-e^{-\lambda})y$ to construct
$\exp\{(1-e^{-\lambda})\sum r_i-\lambda\sum Y_i\}$,
a nonnegative supermartingale, followed by Ville and finite-family
allocation. Release the bound on the longest completely matured origin
prefix; later admitted endpoints contribute at most one each.
Allocate error also across every reported physical history chain and
endpoint. This reports the average conditional risk of executed prefixes;
it does not forecast the next new environment. Several delayed replays
of one participant/session are not independent physical environments.

For $N_j\leq128$ the endpoints are target violation, harmful return,
exceedance $(L_j-X_j)_+/256$, and negative loss $(-D_j)_+/130$.
They are bounded by the stated paid service contract. Empty admission
sets have no coverage rate. Small nonempty sets can give an upper envelope
of one even with no observed failures, and remain uncertified.

\paragraph{Connection to cost-inclusive service.}
For the actual admissions, $D_i\geq X_i$ and $L_i>0$ imply
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf1_{\{D_i<0\}}\leq\mathbf1_{\{X_i<L_i\}},\qquad
 (-D_i)_+\leq(L_i-X_i)_+,\qquad
 \sum_iD_i\geq\sum_iL_i-\sum_i(L_i-X_i)_+.
 \label{eq:conditional-calibration-paid-link}
\end{equation}
The proof is pointwise: a negative $D_i$ forces $X_i<0<L_i$;
$-D_i\leq-X_i<L_i-X_i$ on a negative-return event, and
$D_i\geq X_i\geq L_i-(L_i-X_i)_+$ otherwise and as well.
This connects the same calibrated score to actual negative-return risk
and realized paid return. The common loss ledger additionally bounds
each chain's cumulative negative return pathwise. None of these statements
forces strictly positive net return or superiority over every full-law
competitor without additional decision-value evidence.
